Unseen Action Probe

25 cases the model never trained on: 20 episodes from AgiBot tasks that were in no training split, covering ten action families the training mix barely touches, plus one test episode from each of the 5 held-out tasks. Each case starts from one real frame and is asked three things: the episode's own caption (real), a rewording without arm hints (alt1), and a different action in the same scene (alt2). Three noise seeds each, scored by a VLM judge.

Loop depth

The model was trained with the core blocks looped N = 1–8 times, picked at random per step. Every case, prompt and seed is rolled out at each depth below with the same noise. Pick a depth to switch the headline, the scoreboard and the videos.

Deeper loops do not add capability

On the 13 verifiable cases the real prompt scores 0.87, 0.95, 0.82 and 0.77 at N = 1, 2, 4 and 8. Over all 25 cases it scores 0.60, 0.61, 0.56 and 0.52. Pixel fidelity to the ground truth barely moves (PSNR 15.6 to 15.8, LPIPS 0.32 to 0.31). Each case has 3 seeds, so one clip shifts a case by 0.33, and 0.1 on the verifiable set is about 4 clips. Only the drop at N = 8 holds across prompts.

N = 8 is worst on the held-out tasks

Real-prompt scores on the five unseen tasks fall from 0.93 to 0.87, 0.80 and 0.67 (14 to 10 of 15 clips), mostly in the supermarket case (1.0 to 0.33). The off-script fallback does not go away with depth: the alt2 prompts for base_turn and move_guard stay at 0 to 0.33 at every N.

Depth costs time for nothing

A clip takes 31, 39, 54 and 84 s at N = 1, 2, 4 and 8 on an A100, so N = 8 is 2.7× the cost of N = 1 for a slightly worse result. N = 1 stays the default, in line with the seed sweep.

Headline

The judge misses half of real successes

Shown the real footage and its true caption, Qwen3-VL-32B said completed for 13 of 25 episodes. It misses small or slow motions such as a button press, a cap twist or a slow approach. It almost never accepts a wrong caption. Read a ✓ as reliable and a ✕ as weak evidence, and use the videos for the 12 grey cases.

The scene drives the action more than the words

At N = 1, on the 13 cases the judge can verify, real and reworded prompts score 0.87 and 0.85. A different action in the same scene (alt2) scores 0.59. The real-prompt clips still pass the alt2 instruction 0.67 of the time, so the model mostly does what the scene suggests, and alt2 moves it only part of the way.

Off-script requests fall back to the usual action

At N = 1, three verifiable alt2 prompts scored 0 on all seeds: turn left away from the curtains, squeeze the dressing bottle, and move the guard away from the slicer. Judged against the original caption, those same alt2 clips pass 1.0, 1.0 and 0.67, so the robot did the episode's own action instead. The five held-out tasks (pick, carry, pull) score highest: real 0.93 over 15 clips.

Cases

How the cases were chosen

Why these actions

Pick and place make up 46% of the 160,120 training windows, and fold another 8.6%. The ten families here are the long tail: each is 0.5–5.4% of training windows (tie 0.52%, mix 0.57%, gesture 0.79%, cut/knead 1.2%, up to pour 5.4%), 21.7% together. The 5 held-out tasks are the only tasks the model never saw. Their actions (pick, carry, pull) are common ones, so they test new scenes rather than new actions.

Never in training

Every episode was checked against the train, val and test splits. The 20 family cases are rare actions from tasks that were trained: training took up to 40 episodes from every trainable task, so no unseen task has these actions. These particular episodes were never sampled, fetched or seen. The 5 held-out cases come from the test_unseen split of tasks excluded from training entirely.

Which words it has seen

The real prompt is AgiBot's own caption, and for the family cases that exact sentence appears in the training captions (other episodes of the same task). alt1 and alt2 were written for this eval after looking at the frames and never appear in training, except for two alt2 prompts noted on their cards. For held-out tasks, 3 of 5 captions are new text too.

Context frame

The rollout starts from the first frame of the labelled segment, so the action has not started yet. One tie case has a 13 s segment, longer than the 8.9 s rollout, so partial is the best it can reach.

Generated scenes

No edited images were needed: every family had real never-trained episodes. The pipeline accepts edited context images labelled as generated, and they would show a purple badge here.
